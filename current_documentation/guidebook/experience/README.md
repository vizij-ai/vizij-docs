# Experience

`Experience` is the bucket where a reader first feels Vizij as a working system.

This bucket is the natural home for `Hello Face` and other first-contact experiences that prove the capability quickly with an existing asset and low setup friction.

## Bucket Overview

This bucket should help a reader:

1. encounter a working face quickly,
2. feel visible cause and effect,
3. understand what the quick experience proves and what it does not,
4. step into `Control`, `Customize`, or `Integrate` with the right expectations.

## Depth Ladder

### Surface

**Teaching objective:** deliver an immediate, convincing first encounter with a working face.

**Misunderstandings to prevent:**

1. thinking the first demo is only a video rather than a controllable system,
2. thinking the quick experience already explains how everything works,
3. thinking a richer example requires understanding the whole stack first.

**Often useful content:**

1. sandbox links,
2. embedded demos,
3. screenshots,
4. short videos,
5. concise walkthrough steps,
6. validation checks.

### Fundamentals

**Teaching objective:** explain what the reader is actually interacting with and what parts are prebuilt versus configurable.

**Misunderstandings to prevent:**

1. not knowing what asset or runtime surface is being used,
2. confusing a sample experience with a custom authoring workflow,
3. overgeneralizing from one demo without understanding its boundaries.

**Often useful content:**

1. concept explainers,
2. screenshots,
3. glossary notes,
4. FAQs,
5. implementation bridges,
6. related guidebook links.

### Advanced

**Teaching objective:** deepen the reader's understanding of sample experiences, asset variation, and where exploratory experience gives way to deeper control or customization work.

**Misunderstandings to prevent:**

1. assuming all example experiences are interchangeable,
2. assuming the quick demo surface exposes the entire runtime model,
3. missing where they should transition into `Control` or `Customize`.

**Often useful content:**

1. comparisons,
2. screenshots or videos of multiple sample assets,
3. guided labs,
4. conceptual walkthroughs,
5. implementation bridges.

## Recommended Teaching Approaches

1. Favor embodied proof early: let readers see or touch something real quickly.
2. Use visual and interactive content heavily when it reduces friction.
3. Keep the page honest about what is prebuilt, what is sample content, and what remains to learn.
4. Route readers from first contact into `Control` for understanding or `Integrate` for the quickest next build step.
