# Speaker Notes

Last updated: 2026-03-13

Use these notes with `vizij-afternoon-workshop-deck.html`.

## Slide 1: Title

1. Open with the teaser image or live capstone demo.
2. Tell the room this session is about shipping understanding, not just watching demos.
3. Open with outcomes before architecture.

## Slide 2: Outcomes

1. Read the outcomes as capabilities, not topics.
2. Emphasize that the room will move between authoring, runtime, and deployment.

## Slide 3: Agenda

1. Set expectations for the two support windows and the 5:00 pm breakout.
2. Make the fallback rule explicit: facilitator demos remain the reference path.
3. Tell participants to self-sort into ready-to-run and needs-setup clusters and work in pairs or trios if setup lags.

## Slide 4: Architecture

1. Show how each app has a specific job in the day.
2. Avoid deep implementation detail here; the goal is orientation.

## Slide 5: Authoring

1. Point out that the empty-state sample buttons are a workshop advantage.
2. Ask the room what makes a control reusable versus face-specific.

## Slide 6: Hello Face

1. Use this slide to introduce the runtime-react mental model.
2. Connect the visual screenshot to `VizijAssetBundle`, provider, and face component.

## Slide 7: Renderer Data Model

1. This is the conceptual bridge slide.
2. Keep the explanation short and immediately tie it back to a live path in code.

## Slide 8: Rigging

1. Emphasize that standard inputs are about interoperability.
2. Emphasize that pose weights are about expressive authoring and runtime convenience.

## Slide 9: Support Window

1. Give participants explicit permission to split into troubleshooting versus exploration.
2. Keep unblocked participants moving.

## Slide 10: Animation

1. Use `demo-vizij-player` to explain blending and diagnostics.
2. Keep it conceptual unless the room is already confident.

## Slide 11: Conversation

1. Name the API-key dependency clearly.
2. Make it explicit that this segment is facilitator-led and not a required participant app run.
3. Even if the live demo is limited, use the UI to explain how the control surface is designed.

## Slide 12: Deployment

1. Reframe deployment as a control-surface problem, not a different face problem.
2. Keep the WebSocket example simple and typed.
3. Frame this as guided architecture plus control-path understanding, not participant binary shipping.

## Slide 13: Breakouts and Close

1. Ask each group for one opportunity, one blocker, one next step.
2. Close by tying the workshop back to concrete next actions.
3. Point the room to the guidebook as the main follow-up reading path after the tutorial ends.
